What you can do

A calm, organized place for everything your boat needs.

Stay ahead of the work

Plan ahead. Stay ahead.

Set up oil changes, impeller swaps, zinc rotations, and any other recurring service. Quick-pick chips fill in common intervals. The dashboard surfaces what's overdue, what's in progress, and what's coming up — while everyday "daily basics" stay calm and out of the way.

  • See it coming on a calendar. A month and week view shows what's due, with export to Apple Calendar or Google Calendar.
  • Pull schedules from PDFs. Drop in an owner's manual and let AI extract the maintenance schedule and parts list.
  • Log a whole day at once. Tick off a whole session of finished jobs in one pass, parts and hours included.
  • Map your maintenance locations. Every completed job can carry a location and plots on a chart, with the busiest spots standing out.
  • Leave yourself a note before it's finished. Jot down progress on a job that isn't done yet — it'll be waiting exactly as you left it.
  • Find it without knowing its name. Search turns up the right scheduled task right from the Log Maintenance form, even when you can't remember exactly what it's called.
Parts, stores & provisions

Track every spare part.

Catalog parts by name, part number, and supplier. Ship's Store learns how fast you actually burn through each part and flags what to reorder before your next haul — fast-moving spares rise to the top of the list.

  • Know where everything is. Organize spares by stow location and move them between locations as you use them.
  • Scan a part from its packaging. On-device recognition fills in the manufacturer, name, and part number — no typing.
  • Stock the galley. Track food, water, fuel, and supplies against a par level, or a simple Good, Low, or Out.
  • Provision for a passage. Forecast what you'll need for a trip and build the shopping list for what's short.
Safe & ready to go

Track safety gear.

PFDs, flares, fire extinguishers, EPIRBs, and batteries get their own schedules and expiry reminders — driven by the printed date or an inspection interval. Safety items are interleaved with maintenance by due date on the dashboard, never buried in a separate list.

  • Never miss a renewal. Registration and insurance renewal dates surface in your due list and departure check.
  • Know you're ready to leave the dock. Safety gear, provisions, and overdue service roll up into one departure-readiness score.
Yours alone

Stays on your boat, not our servers.

Your records live on your device — there's no account to make and nothing of yours on a server we run. We don't track you and we don't run analytics. Turn on iCloud Sync and everything stays current across your own Apple devices, and with the crewmates you invite to a boat.

  • Share a boat with your crew. Invite a crewmate to collaborate — private and invite-only, never a public link.
  • Add photos to anything. Attach photos to vessels, parts, and schedules — they sync right along with the record.
  • Works offline. Your records and parsed manuals are always available, with no signal required — only parsing a new manual needs a connection.
How it fits together

Plan a passage, start to finish.

The Trip Planner and Passage Companion are two halves of the same passage: plan it before you leave, then let the app watch it once you're underway — both reading the same galley, so nothing drifts out of sync between them.

  1. 1

    Plan the trip.

    Tell the Trip Planner your crew, your days at sea, and how far you'll be from a resupply. It forecasts every consumable with a use rate against what's aboard and builds a shopping list for anything short.

  2. 2

    Head out, and let Passage Companion take over.

    Tap "We're off" and Passage Companion freezes what's aboard as your departure baseline, then tracks it against how your crew is actually burning through it — no daily logging required. It only speaks up when something's genuinely running short, or it hasn't heard from you in a few days. Fuel is tracked the same way, by engine hours instead of the calendar.

  3. 3

    Bring a crewmate along.

    Share a boat and every entry your crewmate logs syncs straight to your own Ship's Log, attributed to them. A brief notice appears the moment they make a change, wherever you're working in the app — and a durable Partner Activity feed on the Dashboard lets you catch up on their full history any time you open it, not just the instant it happens.

  4. 4

    Handle the one-off jobs and the paperwork.

    A repair that only happens once doesn't need a recurring schedule — create a one-time task with a target date, attach the part it needs, and it lands on your calendar and shopping list like anything else. And when a marina or a border crossing asks for your registration or insurance, it's a couple of taps away, photo included.

Milestones

A trip, a launch, the boat show.

Name what you’re working towards and tag the jobs and purchases that have to happen first. Maintenance Crew keeps count — jobs done, things bought, days to go.

  • Counts you can trust. Jobs done and things bought are tallied separately, so a finished job never hides an unbought life raft.
  • A nudge, three weeks out. If anything’s still open as the day gets close, the Dashboard says so — once.
  • On the Calendar. Dated milestones sit on a Horizon rail and flag their day on the month grid.
  • Next year, in one tap. A finished milestone offers to start next year’s with the same name.
Wishlist

The things you want, not the things you’re due.

A place on the shopping list for the big-ticket and nice-to-have buys — a life raft, collapsible fenders, a new chartplotter — ranked by how much you want them, not by a due date.

  • Grab it, Want, or Someday. Three tiers, set by you. No prices or budgets, by design.
  • Tied to the work. Tag a wish to a Boat Project or a milestone, and the project shows what’s linked and what’s still short.
  • Bought, with Undo. Bought items move into a collapsible group you can put back from.
  • Right on the shopping list. Wishes have their own section next to the parts you’re short, so one trip to the chandlery covers both.
Tanks & trends

Know what's in every tank. See where it's going.

Every tank, mapped

One engine, two tanks, no guessing which is which.

However your fuel and water are actually plumbed — two tanks feeding one engine, a heater that only draws from one of them, a jug you bring along just for a long passage — Maintenance Crew models it the way it really is, not a single tank per category.

  • Map every tank to every system it feeds. A tank can supply more than one system, and a system can draw from more than one tank — set up the Plumbing board once and every reading knows where it belongs.
  • Bring a jug along for the trip. Portable fuel and water containers get their own lifecycle: added aboard for a passage, drawn down or transferred into a fixed tank, then taken off again when you're back.
  • Top up any time — voyage or not. The same simple five-stop level estimate you already use for propane now covers fuel and water too, whether you're mid-passage or topping up at the dock.
  • Pick the right tank without the guesswork. A tightened-up picker on iPhone makes choosing the right tank quick and clear, even with several aboard.
See where it's going

A timeline for everything your boat goes through.

Trends draws one picture from parts, safety gear, fuel, and water — what a schedule already tells you, sitting right alongside what your crew is actually burning through — so you can see whether real life matches the plan.

  • Countdowns you don't have to calculate. Recurring service, registration, insurance, and safety-gear expiries laid out on one timeline, next to what's really been logged.
  • Scheduled, ad hoc, or not yet recorded. Consumption splits into three honest buckets instead of one blurred number, so a trend never gets credit for demand a schedule already explains.
  • Every reading that built an estimate. See the observations behind a learned rate, in order — top-ups, check-ins, and the gaps in between — so a number is a story you can retrace, not just a guess.
  • Honest when there isn't enough history yet. A single reading gets called a first look, not a trend — Maintenance Crew only draws a line once there's enough behind it to earn one.
